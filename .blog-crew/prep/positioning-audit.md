# Positioning Audit

## 1. The arc so far

This blog documents a person finding her lane after years of searching. It starts as a nervous learner's journal — listing curricula, tracking weekly progress, proving effort — and ends with a fully realized identity piece that reframes the wandering as the point. The transformation is genuine and earned, but the middle of the arc is mostly scaffolding that the final post renders obsolete.

## 2. Post-by-post analysis

**2024-10-01 — Welcome to Cognitive Curiosities**
Announces five content pillars (ML techniques, career journey, neurodiversity, AI ethics, curated resources). Signals someone enthusiastic but uncertain, hedging by promising everything. Sets expectations the blog never fulfills — ethics, neurodiversity, and resource curation never get standalone posts. Reads as a mission statement for a blog that became something else.

**2025-02-19 — Learning Log**
The real start. Honest about paralysis, tab overload, imposter syndrome. The Daliana Liu anecdote and the decision to "just start" is the blog's actual origin story. Signals vulnerability and ambition in equal measure. The massive learning list is endearing but also slightly exhausting — it reads like someone trying to outrun self-doubt with volume.

**2025-02-22 — Fluent Python and Special Methods**
A technical tutorial post. Signals she can teach, but the content is entry-level and the framing is still student-mode ("I failed my interview, here's what I'm doing about it"). Honest and useful, but doesn't differentiate her from thousands of junior-dev blogs.

**2025-03-02 — Sunday Summary 1**
Bullet-point progress log. Signals relentless effort. The closing reflection about not recognizing her own progress is the only moment that lifts it above a to-do list. Low signal-to-noise for an outside reader.

**2025-03-16 — Sunday Summary 2**
Same format, same energy. More impressive scope (RAG pipeline, EC2 deployment, Tennis Bot hardware), but still presented as a checklist. No reflection or narrative. A stranger would skim past this.

**2025-04-06 — Tennis Ball Bot Progress**
The first post that reads like a builder writing about building. Technical detail serves a story instead of replacing one. The paragraph about learning how she learns and the closing line about proving something to herself are strong. This is where the voice starts to emerge.

**2026-05-07 — Why I Call Myself an AI Systems Builder**
A different writer than the one who posted the welcome. Confident, reflective, structurally sophisticated. The decade of wandering reframed as accumulated judgment. The shame made explicit and then resolved. This post does real work — it positions her, tells a compelling origin story, and lands on a thesis. It's the piece someone would share. The 13-month gap between post 6 and post 7 is itself the story: she stopped logging and started building.

## 3. Evolution

The shift is dramatic. Posts 1-5 are written by someone who needs the blog to prove she's working hard enough. The voice is earnest, sometimes apologetic, often listing. Post 6 (Tennis Bot) is the pivot — she starts trusting her own experience as material. Post 7 abandons the student frame entirely. The confidence isn't performed; it's backed by specifics. Subject matter moves from "what I'm learning" to "what I've built" to "who I am and why." The gap between posts 6 and 7 suggests the transformation happened in the work, not on the page.

## 4. Gaps in the arc

- **No post covers the actual systems she built at Meta.** Post 7 references them (the evaluation dashboard, the QA add-on) but never shows the thinking. A case-study post would make the "AI systems builder" claim tangible.
- **The consulting practice (Aeterna) is invisible.** The about page mentions it. The blog doesn't. If Aeterna matters to her positioning, it needs at least one post.
- **Nothing about how she thinks about AI, only how she uses it.** Her perspective on AI-human collaboration, on generalists gaining execution speed, on when to trust a model and when not to — these are hinted at in post 7 but never developed. That's where her differentiated thinking lives.
- **The neurodiversity thread was dropped.** Post 1 promises it. Post 2 touches it (the ADHD-driven tab chaos, the notebook system). Then it disappears. Either bring it back with substance or stop promising it.
- **No engagement with other people's ideas.** Every post is self-referential. A response to someone else's argument, a critique of a common take, a book that changed how she thinks — any of these would signal she's in conversation with a field, not just documenting her own journey.

## 5. Stranger test

A stranger landing on the blog today would see: an older "Cognitive Curiosities" brand that doesn't match the current about page, several early posts that read like a junior developer's study journal, and then one standout essay at the end that reframes everything. If they land on post 7, the impression is strong — someone with range, hard-won clarity, and real building experience. If they land on posts 1-5, the impression is a motivated learner, not a builder. The about page does the right work, but the body of content hasn't caught up to who she is now. The blog is currently one great essay surrounded by documentation of an earlier self.

## 6. Next post opportunities

1. **A case study of a system she built at Meta** — makes "AI systems builder" concrete instead of autobiographical. Shows the thinking, not just the title.
2. **Her framework for when AI should (and shouldn't) replace human judgment** — post 7 gestures at this but never develops it. This is where her statistical training and product instinct intersect. High differentiation potential.
3. **What she actually learned from a decade of career changes** — post 7 covers the emotional arc, but a more practical distillation (what transferred, what didn't, what she'd tell someone at year 3 of wandering) would be useful and shareable.
4. **How she uses AI tools to build, specifically** — not a tutorial, but the decision-making layer: when she scaffolds with LLMs, when she doesn't, how she evaluates output, where her judgment overrides the model. This is practitioner knowledge that almost nobody writes about honestly.
5. **A response to a specific idea or argument in the AI space** — positions her as someone with opinions about the field, not just someone building inside it. Could be a reaction to a paper, a product launch, or a trend she thinks is wrong.
