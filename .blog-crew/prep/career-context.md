# Career Context Brief — Kathy Trieu

## Career timeline

- **Pre-2020:** Over a decade cycling through law, journalism, teaching, restaurant management, nonprofit human rights work. BA in Asian Studies. First data science bootcamp ended in discouragement (KNN assignment). Returned to restaurants.
- **COVID era:** Decided to go back. Took calculus at community college with no prior math coursework. Enrolled in and completed M.S. in Statistics at Texas A&M.
- **~Late 2024:** Landed contract role at Meta through Tundra Technical Solutions as a Coding Data Labeling Analyst — reviewing code used to train Llama. Studied MLE by night (LeetCode, textbooks, deep learning specialization, AI book club in LA).
- **Dec 2024:** Interviewed for internal DevOps/data engineering role. Did not get it. Identified it as a turning point regardless.
- **Early 2025:** Deep self-study phase. Tennis ball robot with mechanical engineering friends (YOLO, Raspberry Pi, motion control). Weekly learning logs on the blog. RAG pipelines, Ollama, FastAPI, AWS.
- **Early-mid 2025:** Recognized AI tools had shifted the landscape. Stopped pursuing pure MLE track. Leaned into project management and systems building. Moved into project coordinator role at Meta under a manager who gave her real ownership.
- **Late 2025 - 2026:** Built seven production systems for Meta's Gen AI org — performance evaluation, throughput dashboards, QA tooling, contract worker lifecycle management. 1,200 users, 400-person distributed workforce, 30 PMs. Google Sheets add-on spread organically to other pillars.
- **2026:** Launched Aeterna, a private consulting practice targeting professional services firms (especially PE-backed, post-acquisition). Built the command center app (Next.js + Supabase), public site at buildaeterna.ai, consultant handbook (97 pages). 38 prospects researched. Outreach beginning.
- **2026:** Built Kady, an iPhone app (Expo + Supabase + Claude) for people with ADHD and anxious-avoidant attachment who want to maintain friendships. On-device, 157 tests passing.
- **2026 (current):** Pursuing MATS Autumn 2026 application (due June 7) for AI safety research — thesis on deliberate cognitive constraint as multi-agent design principle ("Spiky Minds").

## Current roles and ventures

- **Day job:** Contractor (Tundra) at Meta, Gen AI org. Title is project coordinator. Actual work: designing and shipping operational infrastructure — dashboards, evaluation systems, data pipelines, LLM integrations. Office Wednesdays.
- **Aeterna:** Bespoke consulting practice. Velocity Audits, operational builds, ongoing stewardship for PE-backed professional services firms post-acquisition. Pricing: $45-90K bundles. Pre-revenue, outreach phase. 4 priority prospects including a warm lead (Aperture).
- **Kady:** Consumer iPhone app. AI relationship coach + cadence tracker for people who care about friends but forget to reach out. Builder is also the primary user. Anti-gamification, anti-social-media.
- **Spiky Minds:** Pre-publication AI safety research. Position paper and LessWrong post forthcoming.

## Key themes and throughlines

- Generalist shame turning into generalist advantage — the emotional core of her writing
- Breadth as infrastructure, not indecision — every past career contributed something structural
- Building without the title — repeatedly doing work that exceeds her role
- AI as equalizer for generalists — not replacing depth, but unlocking people who already had judgment
- Systems thinking applied to human problems (operational visibility, friendship maintenance, workforce management)
- Choosing difficulty after years of avoiding it — the LeetCode-voluntarily moment, the calculus-from-zero moment

## Untold stories (not yet on the blog)

- **The manager relationship.** She references this person repeatedly but has never written about what good mentorship looks like from the receiving end, or what it means when someone gives you room before you've earned it on paper.
- **Building Aeterna while employed at Meta.** The tension of running a consulting practice on the side of a contract role. What it takes operationally, emotionally.
- **Kady as autobiography.** The app is designed for her own ADHD and anxious-avoidant attachment profile. She has not written publicly about that.
- **The 97-page consultant handbook.** She wrote a handbook for a practice with zero clients. That is either disciplined or delusional, and the honest version of that story would be compelling.
- **AI safety research ambitions.** The MATS application, Spiky Minds thesis. A contractor at Meta applying to alignment research programs — that gap is a story.
- **The restaurant years.** She genuinely liked that work. She has never written about what restaurant management taught her about systems, people, or pace.

## Narrative strengths

- Emotional honesty without self-pity. The "AI Systems Builder" post handles shame, loneliness, and career drift without asking for sympathy.
- Concrete specifics. She names numbers (400 workers, 7 systems, 1,200 users), books, algorithms, failures. The writing earns trust through detail.
- Clean structure. She writes in movements — setup, tension, turn, resolution — without forcing a tidy arc.
- She is credible writing about the gap between title and work, between what you are and what people see.

## Narrative risks

- **Aeterna content could read as premature authority.** She has zero consulting clients. Writing about "how to build operational infrastructure for PE firms" before landing one would sound like thought-leadership cosplay. Better to write about the process of starting than the expertise of having arrived.
- **AI-as-great-equalizer framing risks becoming a cliche.** The "AI Systems Builder" post handles it well. Future posts need to stay specific and avoid drifting into "AI empowers everyone" platitudes.
- **ADHD/attachment content could tip into oversharing** if not handled with the same restraint as the career narrative. The blog's strength is precision, not vulnerability for its own sake.
- **Writing about Meta without writing about Meta.** She cannot name internal tools or projects. If posts about her day job stay too vague, they lose the specificity that makes her other writing work. She needs to find the line between discretion and detail.
