# Voice Analysis — Kathy Trieu's Blog

Based on all 7 posts in `_posts/`, from October 2024 through May 2026.

---

## 1. Sentence Patterns

Average sentence length runs long — 20 to 35 words is her comfort zone, especially in reflective passages. But she deploys short sentences for emotional punctuation, and they land hard because the surrounding prose is dense:

- "I was so wrong." (Learning Log)
- "That was painful, because I was asking myself the same thing." (AI Systems Builder)
- "I just moved on to the next thing that came easily." (AI Systems Builder)
- "And somehow, it still doesn't feel like enough." (Learning Log)

She also uses sentence fragments to close a thought with weight: "Full ownership, no title." The pattern is: build up context in a long compound sentence, then cut short. The contrast creates emphasis.

## 2. Punctuation Habits

**Em dashes:** Heavy use, almost always to insert a clarifying aside or pivot mid-sentence. "I've settled on writing about the exercises I complete, incorporating teaching as part of my learning workflow." vs. "I'm taking a different approach—learning at a slower pace." The dash signals a shift in direction rather than elaboration.

**Bold:** Used for two purposes: (a) emphasis on key realizations — "**That is not what I do.**", "**For the first time in my life, I was choosing difficulty, and I loved it.**" and (b) technical terms in the Sunday Summary lists. The first use is distinctly hers; the second is formatting convention.

**Italics:** Reserved for book titles, inner monologue, and imagined speech. "_She's figuring it out, she'll land._" and "_I'm not doing enough._" This is consistent — italics mark thoughts that belong to someone else or to a past self.

**Section breaks (`---`):** Used to mark time jumps or phase transitions in longer posts. In "AI Systems Builder," each `---` moves to a different era of her life. They function as chapter breaks, not decorative spacing.

## 3. Word Choice

Register sits between conversational and precise. She avoids jargon when reflecting ("the ceiling I'd assumed was there didn't actually exist") but uses it freely when it serves specificity ("fine-tuning a YOLOv8n model," "92% recall and over 96% precision"). She never uses jargon to impress — only to be exact.

Colloquial phrasing appears in emotional moments: "which is how you know something had fundamentally changed," "I found it hilarious that they hired me for a job requiring my weaknesses." These read as asides, like she's talking directly to the reader.

No exclamation points in the later posts. The Welcome post (2024) uses them, but by the Learning Log onward, they disappear entirely.

## 4. Emotional Range

She goes fully vulnerable when writing about shame and self-doubt: "The shame of being almost-good-enough at a dozen things and not able to point to a single one and say *this is mine*." These passages are the strongest writing on the blog.

Analytical mode appears in technical posts (Python Special Methods, Sunday Summaries) and in career-strategy passages. The transition between vulnerable and analytical is almost always bridged by a section break (`---`) or a concrete action: she feels something, then she does something about it. She rarely sits in emotion without pivoting to agency.

## 5. Opening Patterns

The strongest opening: "My title is project coordinator. I'm a contractor through Tundra Technical Solutions, stationed at Meta. **That is not what I do.**" — declarative, concrete, immediately creates tension.

The weakest: "Hello! I'm Kathy, and I'm excited to welcome you to **Cognitive Curiosities**." — generic blog-launch copy, exclamation point, "excited." This is the only post that reads like someone else's voice.

Her natural opening move is to start with a specific situation or admission, not a thesis. The Learning Log opens with a confession about procrastination. The Tennis Ball Bot opens with backstory. Both work because they're grounded.

## 6. Closing Patterns

Best closings restate a core idea with new weight: "depth is what finally gave my curiosity somewhere to land." This works because the entire post earned that line.

She also closes with forward-looking statements that feel genuine rather than performative: "Every time I write out a plan, I feel an immediate sense of comfort." The Learning Log ending is honest and small-scale. It doesn't overreach.

The Welcome post closing ("let's navigate this fascinating landscape") is the weakest — it reads like a newsletter template. She has since outgrown this register entirely.

## 7. Voice Drift

Significant drift between the Welcome post (Oct 2024) and everything after (Feb 2025 onward). The Welcome post is formal, stilted, and impersonal. By the Learning Log, her voice has found its actual register: direct, reflective, grounded.

The AI Systems Builder post (May 2026) is the most mature. Sentences are tighter, vulnerability is more controlled, and the structure carries real narrative momentum. The voice has steadily gotten more confident and less hedged over 18 months.

## 8. Tics to Preserve

- **Bold for emotional turning points.** "**That was the first time I proved to myself I could go deep.**" This is her signature emphasis move. It marks the moments she considers defining.
- **Italics for inner monologue.** Past-self thoughts rendered in italics create a dialogue between who she was and who she is now.
- **Short declarative sentences after long buildup.** The rhythm is hers.
- **Section breaks as time skips.** She uses `---` structurally, not decoratively.
- **Direct address sparingly.** "If you're where I was" appears once in the final post — rare, and effective because of it.

## 9. Tics to Flag

- **Over-qualifying.** "however dynamic it may be," "at least for now," "I'm not sure how common it is." These hedges weaken otherwise confident statements. One or two per post is fine; more than that and the voice starts to retreat.
- **List-heavy structure.** The Sunday Summaries and parts of the Learning Log lean on bullet lists as a crutch. Lists work for logging; they flatten narrative posts.
- **"Because" clauses in lists.** The Learning Log's reading/building/practicing section repeats "Because..." to start every explanation. This becomes mechanical.
- **Exclamation points** (Welcome post only). Already gone by post two — just ensure they don't return.

## 10. Style Guide Summary

Kathy's voice is direct, reflective, and grounded in specifics. She writes long, compound sentences that build context, then cuts short for emotional impact. Bold marks defining realizations. Italics mark inner monologue or imagined speech from others. Em dashes redirect mid-sentence. Section breaks (`---`) signal time jumps, not decoration.

Register is conversational-precise: no jargon for show, no hedging for safety. She earns vulnerability by being concrete first — she names the situation, then names the feeling. She never lingers in emotion without pivoting to what she did about it.

Do not add exclamation points. Do not soften her declarative statements with qualifiers. Do not restructure her long-then-short sentence rhythm into uniform lengths. Preserve her bold-for-turning-points pattern. When she is blunt, leave it blunt.

The Welcome post is not the voice. Everything from the Learning Log onward is.
